RAVI HANDA / MONEY, EXPLAINED
Something fairly important is happening in the US
bond market. The yield on the 10 -year
US government bond has crossed 5%.
In fact, on 29th of September, it touched
roughly 5 .3%.
It is the highest level since 2007.
Now, a US government bond yielding 5 %
may sound completely irrelevant to someone sitting in
India, investing in mutual funds, gold or real
estate. But it isn't.
Think of the US Treasury yield as something
close to the gravity of the global financial
markets.
If the US government is willing to give
an investor more than 5 % for 10
years,
every other investment has to answer a single
question.
Why should I take additional risk to own
you?
Before we get into why this is happening,
we need to understand two basic concepts.
The Federal Reserve's policy rate and the US
Treasury yield are not the same thing.
The Fed controls a very short -term interest
rate. Right now, after its September hike, the
target is 3 .75 % to 4%.
But the 10 -year Treasury yield is determined
by the bond market.
If I lend money to the US government
for 10 years, what return do I demand?
That depends upon what I think inflation will
be, what I think the future Fed rates
will be, how strong the economy will be
and how much additional compensation I want for
locking my money away for 10 years.
And remember one basic rule of bonds.
When bond yields go up, existing bond prices
go down. So when you hear that Treasury
yields have shot up, what actually happening is
that investors have been selling bonds or demanding
a higher return before they are willing to
buy them. And this has been a very
large one.
